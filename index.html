Four days at Interlaken.

July 16 to 19, 2026 · Eagle River, Wisconsin · the CAMPSCAPES Heritage & Legacy residency

With the Bridge to the Future campaign underway, these four days build the visual library that donor stewardship, alumni engagement, and recruitment draw on for years. This is the working plan for the residency, and it is a living page: it updates after your team meeting, through the coming week, and through the visit itself.

Send any random thought. Notes, lists, voice memos, half ideas: email them to noah@abba-photo.com any time. It is genuinely helpful. I run everything through an AI intake system that folds it into this plan, so nothing gets lost, and every note helps me get you exactly what you want.

First things first

For your meeting

Please address the following in the course of your meeting. I am not expecting a response before I arrive; these are the things that help me succeed when I get there and give you exactly what you want. I just want them in the room.

  • Shot list one: the spaces that must get full treatment The must, must, must places. Each one gets a graphic frame, an artful frame, a general-purpose frame, and at least one shot ready for publication. The first entries are in from Drea: Lake Finley (waterskiing, sailing, the landscape), K'far Noar with its yurt village and the buildings around it, the Chadar, particularly at Shabbat song session, and Tushball, which I am told I will know when I see it.
  • Shot list two, which is also my introduction Spaces and programs, paired with the feelings they carry: name the place, connect the emotion, Hebrew words welcome. Run it with the community, opened by Toni or Toni's designee, maybe at the end of a meal; I take a quiet room afterward for whoever has more to say. Hearing it together gives the exercise its weight, it tells me exactly what matters here, and it makes my presence in program make sense from the first meal.
  • Key moments on the schedule If something matters at a particular hour in a particular place, tell me and it goes on the plan.
  • Word to staff about my hours I will be creeping about at night and early morning. 11pm to 7am is my normal window; I rest during high sun.
  • Day one My arrival time, media releases on file, and who walks the grounds with me first.

The work

My approach

This is my understanding from my conversations with Drea. If any line is wrong, that is exactly what I want to hear about.

  • The thirty plus candids capture the warmth of the program, highlight engagement and communal experience You told me what you want: the warmth in the program, not faces lined up for a camera. So I photograph campers and staff inside the activity, where the connection shows. It is the way I already work.
  • The twelve Campscapes come from your must-list You name the places that carry Interlaken. Each one gets full treatment: a graphic frame, an artful frame, a general-purpose frame, and at least one shot ready for publication.
  • The campaign crosses the bridge Your language arrived: the bridge is the icon of continuity, and the most meaningful crossing happens at the start of Shabbat, the whole camp walking together to light candles. That crossing gets photographed like the signature it is. So does the bridge alone: at first light, at night, with the water moving beneath it. The constant, while everyone who crosses it changes.
  • The dining hall gets recorded before it changes A gift is renovating it. Before the room changes, its warmth goes on record: I search your full archive for the frames that already hold it, and I photograph it as it stands while I am there.
  • The media team leaves this with a system A 45-minute setup, recurring 15-minute check-ins, and a written field guide. The goal is a team and a process with a minimum of 200 images ready for upload, every day.

The trip

How the four days run

The agreement includes 24-hour access to campus; dawn and night frames are the reason. The shape below is set; the hour-by-hour flexes with weather and the program day.

Thu Jul 16
Arrive, walk the grounds, start shooting A walk-through against the priority list with my point person, then coverage begins the same afternoon. Evening program, and the first night sky if it is clear.
Fri Jul 17
Full day, into Shabbat First light before wake-up, programs through the day, and then the whole camp crosses the bridge together into Shabbat. That crossing is the campaign's central image and it happens once while I am here; the evening is built around it.
Sat Jul 18
Full day two The unhurried day. Daily life, the waterfront, the places on the campaign list, golden hour, night work after dark.
Sun Jul 19
Last light, wrap Morning coverage, any frames the list still needs, and out.

The program

The training

The goal is a team and a process that has a minimum of 200 images ready for upload, every day. The pieces below build it, and all of them are covered.

Media team setup · 45 minutes

One working session with the media team: how to find the engagement in a frame, how the library gets used, images that make stakeholders say "That's MY camp!"

15-minute check-ins, recurring

Short scheduled check-ins with the media team after setup. The 45 minutes puts the system in place; the check-ins are what keep it working.

The photographers' guide

A written guide the team keeps: the system from the setup session, and how to get the most out of the equipment camp already owns. Read the edition CYJ's team shoots with this summer; Interlaken's gets tuned to your Canon kit and your workflow.

Gear rehab

The camp's kit is in from Drea: Canon T5, T6, and T7 bodies with the wide and telephoto zooms they came kitted with, and now a 50mm f/1.8 prime the camp just added. That fast little lens is what turns dim dining halls and evening programs into images that hold up. I go through each body and lens and get them back into working shape for the people who use them.

The setup session lands during the residency; the check-in cadence gets scheduled with the media team directly.
