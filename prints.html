The photographs, working for the camp

ConcerningThis summer's photographs, and every photograph the camp has
Valid throughDecember 31, 2026

The residency left the camp a library of photographs organized by use, a book layout, and a page that holds them. This proposal sets out five ways to put them to work this year. Each line stands on its own, priced on its own, engaged on its own. The camp takes what it needs, when it needs it.

1The store, for families and alumni

What it is

Prints of this summer's photographs on archival paper, by the lab that prints my books, shipped to the buyer's door. Families and alumni order from the camp's page. Each frame is offered only at the sizes it can honestly print.

Prices to the buyer

Open edition, 16x20$250
Open edition, 20x30$450
Open edition, 24x36$700
Limited edition, the night frames, 16x20 to 30x45$450 to $1,500
Framing, when wantedat the lab's cost, quoted first

What the camp receives

20% of every sale, paid quarterly with the list of what sold. No cost to the camp. Quality and production are mine: a print that arrives wrong is replaced.

2The auction set

What it is

Six framed limited editions, chosen together from the wall pieces on the camp's page: fine-art paper, a black metal frame, and a provenance card with each, naming the place, the date, and the edition. The catalog copy and a photograph of each lot come with the set, delivered to the JCC before the event.

Investment

Each piece, framed$549
The set of six$3,294

The camp keeps everything the room gives.

At earlier camps, prints like these have sold at auction for $1,000 and more.

For KidShare in May, items gather from January. If the campaign holds an event this fall, the set can be ready for it.

3The whole library, curated

What it is

Every photograph the camp has, from every summer and every source, gathered into one library: duplicates removed, dated, organized by what each image is for, in the camp's own Drive, with a one-page report of what the appeal, the enrollment page and the feed still lack.

Alumni by era: the decades, the bunks, the faces the community knows, pulled and curated from that library for reunions, the alumni appeal, and then-and-now on the feed.

I can curate images from however big the library is.

Investment

Review and curate up to 4,000 photographs$2,500
Review and curate up to 12,000 photographs$4,500
Larger librariesquoted first

Curation is the review, the selection and the organization. Retouching and print preparation are not included; they are quoted per frame when a use calls for them.

4Stewardship gifts

What it is

Thank-you kits for donors and alumni, with the camp's photographs inside: a Shabbat box, a s'mores kit. I assemble the pieces and send them as kits. The development team puts them together and hand-delivers, or ships them to alumni far from Milwaukee, a group at a time. An afternoon of thank-yous is the kind of work a development team does together.

Just doing thank-yous is huge motivation for a development team.

Investment

Each kit is quoted first, by what goes in it and how many.

5Consultation, by the hour

What it is

Before the campaign asks, the feed has to have earned the attention. I work with whoever runs the camp's channels, by the hour, to make the photographs do that work on a date.

For a December ask, that means an eight-week run-up planned to the day: which frames, in what order, what each post says and when it goes. Every comment answered, and how, with the reply that opens a conversation. Engagement tracked week by week, and every reply, share and tag followed up by a person before the ask lands. The ask goes out to people who have been looking for two months.

Investment

Consultation$250 an hour
A campaign run-up, usually10 to 15 hours across eight weeks

7Terms

Each line is engaged on its own, by a one-page agreement naming what is delivered and when. Invoices are payable within 30 days. Framing, kit materials and larger libraries are quoted before any work begins. Prices hold through December 31, 2026. The price is the price.

The store sells only frames the camp releases, in writing. Camp families are the camp's relationship and are routed through the camp's page.

8How it starts

The camp names the frames it releases and the line to begin with. The store goes on the camp's page. The auction set is chosen together from the wall pieces. The library starts with whatever the camp can hand me, in any state. The run-up starts eight weeks before the ask.
